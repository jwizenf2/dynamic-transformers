## 2. What a per-request loop controller must beat

The comparisons below are the strongest ones published for C4. All of them are on Ouro unless noted.

1. **Ouro's own gate (Q-exit) at a tuned q.** This is the baseline C2 targets.
   - Ouro paper, MMLU: about 66% at 2.5 average rounds vs 67.35% at 4. That is about 37% fewer rounds for about −1.4 pts.
   - 2607.20519, measured on an RTX 5070 Ti with Ouro-1.4B: 1.24–1.58× speedup at −0.5 to −1.6 pts (ARC-E 1.58×, −1.6 pts; ARC-C 1.51×, −0.7 pts).
   - 2608.18171, NESTful with Ouro-2.6B: matches fixed-depth-4 Win Rate with fewer per-token iterations. No numbers in the text.
   - *Implication:* C2's "≈10% of oracle saving" is measured differently (fraction of the oracle's reachable saving, per request, on generative tool calls). The paper must state that metric explicitly and report the plain "average loops at matched accuracy" metric that these papers use. Otherwise reviewers will read the two results as contradictory.
2. **Training-free post-hoc readouts** (2607.20519): hidden cosine, hidden L2, max prob, margin, entropy, chosen on validation. They beat the gate in 5 to 8 of 12 settings, depending on which count in the paper is used. Example: OBQA with hidden cosine reaches 1.99× measured. A per-request router must beat the *best of these readouts*, not just the gate.
3. **Convergence/KL exits.** Huginn's KL < 5e-4 (MT-Bench 5.56 vs 5.63). The second-order step-size rule (2509.23314). Ouro's ‖Δh‖ threshold, which lands within 1–2 pts of the trained gate on MMLU.
4. **Ragged-KV Ouro + gate** (2610.09013). At τ=0.5, Ouro-1.4B gets 62.9 vs 64.2 at 3.05/4 depth, and 2.6B-Thinking gets 62.3 vs 62.1 at 2.83/4. Measured throughput is 1.64× at batch 1 but **below baseline at batch 32**.
5. **LoopRPT RL-fine-tuned exit** (2603.19714). Ouro-2.6B Hard split: 37.24 at 2.28 steps vs base gate 34.35 at 3.51. Measured latency −39.5% at 4k context. This is the strongest *trained* baseline, but it changes the weights. A frozen controller should be compared with it on cost as well as accuracy.
6. **Fixed best depth per task/dataset** (a static oracle). This is trivially strong at T_max=4 and must always be reported.
7. **Per-query/sequence controllers from other settings**: the Damani et al. predictor (up to 50% compute cut, for best-of-k), the Dr.LLM routers (+3.4 pts with about 5 layers saved), and the 2410.20022 sequence oracle (23.3% of layers). These are the natural off-the-shelf learned-router baselines to adapt to loop count.
8. **Bandit exit selectors** (UEE-UCB, UCBEE, UAT). Context-free UCB over loop counts is the minimum bandit baseline for C4.

## 3. Threats to novelty

- **C2 (gate doesn't track need):**
  - 2607.20519 (same group as 2608.18171) already diagnoses Ouro's gate on frozen checkpoints and finds it "not uniformly Pareto-optimal", with post-hoc readouts often better.
  - 2610.09013 argues the per-token uniform prior makes exits homogeneous across tokens, which is a mechanistic explanation that pre-empts part of C2.
  - Novelty that remains for C2: (i) generative tool calling, (ii) per-request granularity, (iii) explicit **oracle** comparison (neither paper reports Ouro oracle numbers), (iv) the conditional analysis against dependency depth.
  - Risk: 2608.18171 reports the gate *works well* on NESTful, so C2 must be framed as "leaves most oracle headroom" and not as "fails".
- **C4 (structure-aware per-request controller):**
  - LoopRPT is RL with a difficulty-aware compute penalty on Ouro's exit, but per-token and with fine-tuning.
  - RL-Halting is per-example REINFORCE halting, but synthetic and trained from scratch.
  - ALoDLM uses REINFORCE halting plus a depth prior.
  - The bandit early-exit literature (UEE-UCB, UCBEE, UAT; LEE/DEE contextual-bandit exits [SRCH]) covers the bandit formulation.
  - Dr.LLM, Glavas et al. and the Elbayad sequence-level variant cover per-example, oracle-supervised depth routing on frozen models.
  - Damani et al. cover per-query compute allocation.
  - The novel combination is **per-request loop count for a frozen looped LM, with features from pass 1 / the tool-chain structure, on tool calling, with an oracle-gap metric**. No single paper found does all of these. Searches for "pass-1 probe predicts Ouro loops" found nothing direct. 2607.18553 (probes on Ouro-RLTT) and 2609.01924 ("difficulty maps onto number of loops"; "Ouro … mostly done by loop 3") [SRCH] are adjacent and should be read.
- **C1 / C3 adjacent:**
  - 2604.07822 shows depth extrapolation with hop count, plus overthinking.
  - TaH documents latent overthinking (8.7% of tokens fixed vs 2.1% broken at pass 2).
  - STARS documents the collapse beyond the peak depth.
  - Huginn shows per-category step differences.
  - C3's "shallow requests lose and deep chains gain beyond T_max" is consistent with these, but not pre-empted for tool calling in our search.

## 4. Known dead ends and pitfalls

1. **Ouro's early exit saves no compute as shipped.**
   - The HF implementation computes all passes (default early_exit_threshold=1.0) [SRCH via 2607.20519], and vLLM runs all 4 loops [SRCH, project page].
   - 2610.09013: each depth needs its own KV rows, so exited tokens are still run to full depth.
   - Ouro paper: reusing the KV cache in prefill costs more than 10 pts on GSM8K.
   - → Report loop-count savings as **FLOP/loop estimates**. Measure latency only for per-request depth, which needs no ragged KV, and say so.
2. **Per-token exit plus batching kills wall-clock gains.**
   - 2610.09013's throughput is *lower* than fixed depth at batch 32 (773.2 vs 807.9 tok/s).
   - DREX gets only +2–12%.
   - CDB recovers 1.32–1.53×, but prefill (3.7–36% of GPU time) caps end-to-end gains, and step latency is flat in the memory-bound regime.
   - EE-LLM notes that KV recomputation relies on batching effects.
   - Per-request depth sidesteps ragged batches. This is an argument for C4, provided requests are bucketed by depth.
3. **KV-hole / missing-state problem.**
   - CALM and 2407.20272 copy states, and FREE shows quality loss even under the oracle with state copying.
   - River-LLM: a theoretical 3.3× collapses to a marginal gain.
   - CHASE: severe perplexity degradation for state-space looped models without adaptation.
4. **Oracle headroom vanishes for layer early exit in modern dense LLMs.**
   - 2603.23701: oracle skip ratios of 0–7%.
   - This does not carry over directly to looped models. Ouro's per-pass readouts are trained, and 2/3/4 passes give 35.8/60.5/64.2 macro on 1.4B (2610.09013). The headroom there is real.
5. **Gates trained jointly with a uniform/entropy prior homogenise depth** (2610.09013). Joint gate training shapes the trajectory, which is the root of gate failures (2607.20519). Simple confidence readouts often match learned gates.
6. **Routers learn length, not difficulty.**
   - LayerRoute's headline result was withdrawn: the router tracked sequence length.
   - RL-Halting finds a spurious length ↔ loop-count correlation.
   - → C1/C2 must control for length and number of calls, as the project already plans.
7. **Overthinking / beyond-trained-depth collapse** (TaH, STARS, 2604.07822, Bansal 2022). More loops are not monotone, so "max loops" is not an upper bound on accuracy and the oracle must take the argmax over depths, not the first correct one.
8. **Learned stochastic stopping can stabilise a suboptimal computation.** RL-Halting on Copy: 43.2 vs 68.7 for K=n. RL controllers need a fixed-depth sanity baseline.
