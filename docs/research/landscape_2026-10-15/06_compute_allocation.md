# 06 — Per-request test-time compute allocation outside looped models

Survey date: 2026-10-15. Scope: baselines, borrowable methods, and novelty threats for C4
(a per-request, structure-aware controller that picks the number of Ouro loop passes for
tool calling). Our current router: logistic predictors on pass-1 hidden states plus
gate/entropy/margin features and LinUCB replay. It captures about 37% of the oracle compute saving.

Verification: every arXiv ID below was opened on arxiv.org (or the venue page for non-arXiv
items) during this survey, and the numbers come from those abstracts or pages. Items marked
**[unverified]** rely on search snippets or memory and were not checked against the primary page.
"n/r" means the abstract reports no number.

## Methods worth borrowing for our controller

1. **Warm-started LinUCB plus a knapsack budget (PILOT, 2508.21141).** Use our offline logistic predictors as the LinUCB prior mean (the preference-prior idea). The regret bound improves when the prior is close to the truth. Add an online multi-choice-knapsack (ZCL) policy so a *fleet-level pass budget* is met across requests, rather than only a per-request λ penalty.
2. **λ-conditioned single policy (BaRP, 2510.07429).** Feed the compute-penalty weight as an input to the controller (policy gradient with entropy regularization and calibrated cost scaling). One trained model then produces the whole accuracy-vs-passes curve. Compare against LinUCB/TS/ε-greedy as BaRP does.
3. **Predict marginal gain, not difficulty (Damani 2410.04707).** Regress Δaccuracy(k→k+1) per request and allocate greedily under a budget. This handles "beyond trained max" naturally: continue while predicted marginal gain exceeds λ·cost.
4. **Minimum-sufficient-compute labels and boundary taxonomy (Ares 2603.07915, BAAR 2602.21227).** Label each request with the smallest pass count that solves it. Use always-min / always-max boundary runs to split requests into {easy, needs-more, max-helps, unsolvable}. Train only on the solvable portion, or give "unsolvable" an explicit "spend minimum" arm, which saves compute that an oracle saves too.
5. **Monotone curve fitting for supervision (Handoff Curves 2610.15993).** Replace noisy per-pass correctness with an isotonic/monotone fit of success vs. passes per request (or per cluster). They report +0.2 to 4.1 pp at equal cost from this alone. Non-monotonic Ouro pass curves (overthinking) should be handled explicitly, not smoothed away.
6. **Search-derived labels and router losses (Dr.LLM 2510.12773).** Focal loss with class balancing (pass-count classes are imbalanced), windowed mean-pooling of hidden states, and bottleneck MLP heads. Their MCTS label generation maps to exhaustive pass enumeration, which is cheap for us with only about 4–6 arms.
7. **Cheap-draft agreement features (DART 2606.23181; Manvi 2410.02725).** Agreement between pass-1 and pass-2 decoded tool calls (or two cheap samples), and draft entropy, as training-free signals. DART-style agreement is the most important training-free baseline to beat.
8. **Residual-delta features (DiffSkip; Dr.LLM; Routing Plateau's "partial trajectories").** ‖h_k − h_{k−1}‖, cosine(h_k, h_{k−1}), and the logit-lens change of the first tool name and argument tokens across passes. The Routing Plateau paper argues query-only features plateau and partial-trajectory signals are needed.
9. **Layer selection for probes (Prefill Activations 2603.20895).** Choose which pass/layer to probe by Fisher separability rather than using only the final pass-1 state.
10. **Structure features (2605.25310; Adaptive-RAG 2403.14403; HAB 2606.01168).** Linear probes for predicted dependency depth / number of calls, plus cheap request-text features (number of candidate tools, nesting cues, conjunctions). Use predicted depth as a controller input and report its standalone AUROC. Note 2605.25310's finding that the signal vanishes in single-shot planning, so test whether pass-1 states encode depth before any call is emitted.
11. **Evaluation protocol.**
    - (a) Accuracy-vs-mean-passes curves swept over λ, with fixed-k baselines (k = 1..max and beyond).
    - (b) Report the *oracle gap*, defined two ways:
      - % of the oracle compute saving captured at matched accuracy (our current ~37%);
      - % of the best-fixed-k-to-oracle accuracy gap closed at matched compute (RouteLLM-style APGR; Prefill-Activations reports 45.6%).
    - (c) Mandatory simple baselines: always-k, random with a matched mean, a confidence/entropy threshold, kNN (2505.12601), DART-style agreement, and Ouro's own learned exit gate.
    - (d) Stratify by dependency depth (NESTful, FuncBenchGen 2509.26553) to show the passes chosen rise with depth.
    - (e) Measure wall-clock and FLOPs separately (FlexiDepth and LayerRoute show tiny real speedups).
    - (f) Calibration and OOD transfer (TAB trains on MATH and transfers to BFCL; Dr.LLM reports OOD drop).

## Threats to novelty

- **2608.18171 (Looped LMs improve compositional tool calling).** Already shows adaptive loop depth on BFCL/NESTful with the largest gains on dependency tasks. C4 must differ in a request-level *learned controller with explicit compute penalty, structure features, and beyond-max extrapolation*, plus an oracle-gap analysis. (Covered in the project's novelty check.)
- **TAB (2604.05164).** RL budget policy for agentic tool use (BFCLv4 multi-turn) under a global budget. Its All-SubQ variant conditions budgets on the known plan, which comes close to "structure-aware". It differs from C4 in allocating CoT tokens per turn on a non-looped solver.
- **Ares (2603.07915) and BAAR (2602.21227).** Per-step effort/model routing in agents with minimum-sufficient labels. "Learned per-step compute selection for agents" is not new. Our novelty has to rest on *latent depth (loops)* and *dependency-structure conditioning*.
- **HAB (2606.01168) and Adaptive-RAG (2403.14403).** Predict reasoning depth or hop complexity *before solving* and allocate compute accordingly. "Predict structural depth, then allocate" is established for CoT steps and retrieval hops, but we found nothing for tool-call dependency depth or loop passes.
- **Dr.LLM (2510.12773), MACRO (2608.05872), 2610.04751.** Per-example routing that can *repeat* layers on dense LLMs, i.e. learned per-input extra depth. A reviewer may see C4 as "Dr.LLM for Ouro". The distinctions: a natively looped model, whole-stack passes, tool-call structure, bandit/compute-penalty formulation, and extrapolation past the trained max.
- **Bandit routers (PILOT, MixLLM, BaRP, 2506.17670) and bandit early exit (UCBEE, CeeBERT).** LinUCB for *depth/exit* has precedent on BERT/CNNs. LinUCB for LLM *model* choice is common. We found no LinUCB/contextual-bandit work selecting *loop count in a looped LM* (search for "contextual bandit exit depth LLM" returned only model routing). Treat this as a gap, still provisional.
- **Handoff Curves (2610.15993), posted 4 days ago.** Covers per-request vs. per-step routing on BFCL. Its finding supports our per-request design but leaves less room to claim the "initial selection suffices" insight.

## Known dead ends / cautionary results

- **Routers plateau far below oracle with query-only features.** The Routing Plateau (2606.07587) tested 21 routers including kNN: they cluster in a narrow band, and more data, larger encoders, and fine-tuning add only about 1.24 pp. Our ~37% oracle-saving capture is in line with this. Gains likely need richer trajectory features (pass-2 deltas, decoded-call agreement), not bigger classifiers.
- **Simple baselines often win.** Well-tuned kNN matches or beats learned routers (2505.12601). A self-confidence gate beats a frozen LLM router at fewer tokens, and a tier-majority baseline beats learned routers in an ablation (2608.14927; the latter from a search snippet). Failure is predictable (AUROC 0.885) but *which* extra compute helps is not (AUPRC 0.10–0.17). By analogy, "will pass 1 be wrong" may be easy while "will exactly 3 passes fix it" is hard.
- **Routing/mode collapse.** RL routers collapse to one mode when there is no accuracy gap to learn from (2608.20256 on Countdown; Thinkless needs decoupled GRPO to avoid it). A compute-penalized RL controller over pass counts is likely to collapse to always-min or always-max on homogeneous splits.
- **Learned adaptive depth degenerating to a static pattern.** In LayerRoute (2609.13682), the same 9 layers are skippable across all seeds and wall-clock speedup is only 1.02–1.06x. FlexiDepth reports no wall-clock speedup. Per-request decisions should be checked for real input-dependence (entropy of chosen k, mutual information with depth) and for real latency.
- **Mid-trajectory handoff not worth it on BFCL.** Later handoff did not beat initial selection on BFCL or MultiWOZ and raised cost on BFCL (2610.15993). This argues against complex per-step re-decisions for tool calling.
- **Confidence-based selection leaves headroom.** 2610.04751 notes confidence-based route selection leaves potential untapped. A plain confidence threshold is a strong baseline but not the ceiling.
