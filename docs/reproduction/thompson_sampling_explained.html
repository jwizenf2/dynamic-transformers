Which attention heads does DHT drop, and how is that decided?

A plain-language and code-level explanation of the Thompson sampling that picks the attention heads in our Dynamic Hierarchical Transformer reproduction (Meng et al., 2024).

Every snippet below is copied from the repository with its file and line. The worked example is a real decision made by our trained T-DHT model on AG News.

Short answer. It is contextual, combinatorial (top-K) Thompson sampling with a Bayesian logistic-regression reward model for each head. Each head's posterior is kept as a diagonal Gaussian updated by an online Laplace approximation. This is the logistic Thompson sampling of Chapelle & Li (2011), extended to choose K heads at once.

Said as one sentence for a slide: each attention head is an arm with its own logistic model of "is keeping this head good for this input?"; for every sample and every 3-layer group we draw one parameter vector per head from its approximate Gaussian posterior, score all 12 heads with a sigmoid, keep the K best, then update only the kept heads with one shared 0/1 label derived from the batch reward.

Where it sits in DHT

BERT-base has 12 layers with 12 attention heads each. DHT always runs the first 3 layers in full (the prefix). It then treats layers 3–5, 6–8 and 9–11 as three groups. For every sample, two separate decisions are made at each group the sample reaches:

  • How many heads (K). Tree search (T-DHT) asks a LinUCB bandit per group (8, 10 or 12). Integrated search (F-DHT) fixes K once from its (depth, heads) pair.
  • Which K heads. This is the Thompson sampler described here. The same K heads, at their original positions, are used in all three layers of the group.

So Thompson sampling never chooses how much to prune, only which heads survive once K is known.

The algorithm, step by step

  1. Build a feature vector for every head

    For head h in group g the bandit sees a 24-number vector w_h = [context ; descriptor_h]:

    • context (12 numbers): the sample's hidden state entering this group, mean-pooled over its real tokens (1 sequence bin × 4 + 8 embedding bins) and standardised with training-set statistics. Identical for all heads.
    • descriptor_h (12 numbers): computed once from 512 training samples before training. It is the average pooled representation in head h's 64-dimensional slice of the hidden vector after this group. Different per head and per group.

  2. Start every head with the same prior

    Each head keeps a posterior mean m_h and a diagonal precision q_h (both 24-vectors). They start at m = 1, following the paper's notation, and q = 1, which is our repair of the paper's singular 0. There is one set of 12 head models shared by all three groups; the groups differ only through their contexts and descriptors.

  3. Draw one sample per head, score, keep the top K

    For each (sample, group) decision the sampler draws fresh parameters for all 12 heads at once, scores each head, and keeps the K highest:

    θ̃_h ~ N( m_h , diag(1 / q_h) ) for h = 0 … 11 score_h = σ( θ̃_hᵀ w_h ) keep = top-K heads by score_h

    During training this draw is the exploration. At inference (explore=False) there is no draw: the posterior mean m_h is used, so the choice is deterministic.

  4. Run only the kept heads

    The controller calls the sampler once per group and executes those heads in all three layers. Inside attention, only the kept heads' rows of the Q, K and V weights (and the matching output columns) are multiplied, so dropped heads cost no matrix work at all. They are not computed and then zeroed.

  5. Turn the batch loss into one 0/1 label

    After the optimizer step, each sample's loss is multiplied by a cost: the head count K for the tree group, or depth × heads for integrated search. It is then compared with the previous batch's average:

    r = mean_prev_batch(loss × cost) / (loss × cost) y = 1 if r ≤ 1 else 0

    The ratio is capped at 100, and the rule for y is the paper-literal one printed on p. 444. That single label y is given to every kept head of that group. Unkept heads are not updated at all.

  6. Update each kept head's posterior (the Laplace step)

    Each kept head solves a tiny Bayesian logistic regression with one new observation, using its current posterior as the prior. The new mean is the MAP estimate. The precision grows by the logistic curvature at that point, times the squared features (element-wise, so the covariance stays diagonal):

    m_h ← argmin_θ ½ Σ q_h (θ − m_h)² + softplus(θᵀw_h) − y·θᵀw_h q_h ← q_h + σ(m_hᵀw_h) · (1 − σ(m_hᵀw_h)) · w_h²

    The MAP is found with damped Newton steps and an Armijo line search. The precision update is exactly the form printed in the paper, and it is the update in Chapelle & Li's regularized logistic regression.

A real decision from our trained model

This is the frozen T-DHT checkpoint on AG News test sentence #0 (true class Business). The depth bandit sent it through all 12 layers. For layers 3–5, the head-count bandit scored 8 heads at +9.17, 10 heads at −2.03 and 12 heads at +4.77, so K = 8. Thompson sampling then ranked the heads by their posterior-mean scores:

“Fears for T N pension after talks. Unions representing workers at Turner Newall say they are ‘disappointed’ after talks with stricken parent firm Federal Mogul.”
kept (8): heads 0, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 10, 11dropped (4): heads 1, 3, 8, 9

The four dropped heads are exactly the four with the lowest scores (0.338–0.349). Two things are worth pointing out:

  • The scores are tightly bunched. All 12 lie between 0.338 and 0.365, and the axis above is zoomed in to show them. After three epochs the posterior means barely separate the heads, so during training the random draw, not the mean, usually decides which heads are dropped. At inference, small differences still decide deterministically.
  • Every score is below 0.5. Under the paper-literal label rule, a label of 1 means "this batch was less efficient than the last one" (see below). The scores are therefore relative rankings, not calibrated "probability this head helps".

Which flavor of Thompson sampling, precisely

QuestionAnswer in this implementation
Contextual?Yes. The choice depends on the sample's current hidden state and on per-head descriptors.
Reward modelBernoulli with a logistic link, one independent model per head.
PosteriorGaussian approximation with diagonal covariance, updated online (Laplace: MAP mean, Hessian-diagonal precision).
Samples per decisionOne draw of all 12 heads' parameters per (sample, group), from a private, checkpointed RNG.
ActionTop-K superarm (combinatorial). K comes from the count bandit (tree) or the chosen pair (integrated).
FeedbackOne binary label per superarm, copied to every kept head. Semi-bandit-style update, without per-head credit assignment.
InferenceNo sampling: rank by the posterior mean.
Often assumedWhat it actually is
Beta–Bernoulli TS per headNo. That has no context; ours conditions on the input.
Linear-Gaussian TS (Agrawal & Goyal)No. The reward is logistic, so the posterior is approximate, not conjugate.
Exact Bayesian posteriorNo. It is an online diagonal Laplace approximation, and feature correlations are ignored.
TS over all C(12, K) subsetsNo. Per-head draws followed by top-K, which keeps 495 possible 8-head subsets tractable.

What the paper specifies, and what we had to choose

§3.2 gives the sampling, the sigmoid scores, the top-K selection and the precision update. Three pieces could not be implemented as printed. Decision D005 in docs/project/decisions.md records each repair:

  • Prior precision printed as 0. That makes the first draw undefined (infinite variance). We use 1.
  • The printed MAP objective has a positive quadratic term inside a maximization, and its likelihood term vanishes when y = 0. We use the standard penalized logistic loss shown above.
  • Diagonal vs full covariance is ambiguous. The update squares features element-wise, which implies a diagonal; the initialization is printed as a matrix. We keep the diagonal.
The label direction contradicts the paper's own text. The printed rule (p. 444) sets y = 1 when r ≤ 1, i.e. when the sample was costlier or lossier than the previous batch average. The prose says 1 should mean "more effective". Our main runs follow the printed rule for fidelity, and the corrected rule (y = 1 when r > 1) is available as feedback_rule = "improvement". A bounded two-epoch check gave similar accuracy under both (.8707 vs .8723 macro F1 on 512 validation samples), so the question is still open, and it is a fair point to raise with the paper.

Caveats worth raising

  • Shared credit. All kept heads get the same label, so a head that hurt the prediction is rewarded alongside the ones that helped. Per-head effects are never observed.
  • The descriptor is a slice, not a head output. Head h's 64-dimensional slice of the hidden vector after a full layer group has been mixed by the output projection and the feed-forward block, so it identifies a position, not what head h computed. The paper describes the descriptor the same way ("split each vector into sub-matrices along the embedding dimension").
  • Non-stationary reward. The label compares against the previous batch's average, and the network changes underneath the bandit. The reward ratio hit its cap of 100 about 81,500 times in the full T-DHT run.
  • Weak separation. As the example shows, posterior means end up close together. Which heads get dropped is therefore largely exploratory noise during training, and our two search modes do not agree on which heads are unimportant.
